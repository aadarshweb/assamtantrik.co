About Kamakhya Temple: The Shakti Peetha of Assam

Kamakhya Temple, located atop Nilachal Hill in Guwahati, Assam, is one of the 51 Shakti Peethas, the most sacred sites of goddess worship in Hinduism. According to the Kalika Purana</em>, this is the spot where the yoni</em> (womb) of the goddess Sati fell to earth after Lord Vishnu used his Sudarshana Chakra to dismember her body. This divine origin gives Kamakhya Temple a unique cosmological significance unmatched anywhere in India.

The temple complex is not just a place of devotion. It is the living epicenter of Tantrik Hinduism. Kamakhya temple tantriks have practiced here continuously since the 8th century CE. The Ambubachi Mela, held annually in June, draws hundreds of thousands of devotees and is called the "Tantrik Kumbh Mela" because it is the single largest gathering of genuine tantrik in kamakhya temple practitioners anywhere in the world.

Visiting Kamakhya Temple: Practical Notes

The temple sits on Nilachal Hill in Guwahati and the surrounding area is a working religious site rather than a tourist attraction, so ordinary temple etiquette applies: modest dress, shoes removed before entering the sanctum, and photography only where it is explicitly permitted. The main shrine closes for ritual periods, and the complex has its own schedule during the Ambubachi Mela in June, when the city fills with visitors and access is slower than usual.

Guwahati is well connected by rail and air, and the Kamakhya area is reachable from the main city by road. Why is Kamakhya Temple called a Shakti Peetha?

+

The Kalika Purana records that the yoni of the goddess Sati fell here after Vishnu dismembered her body with the Sudarshana Chakra. Kamakhya is therefore one of the 51 Shakti Peethas, the most sacred sites of goddess worship in Hinduism, and is the supreme seat of Shakti.

+
